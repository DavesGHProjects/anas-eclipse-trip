Day 8 — Following the Family Name into Asturias

Sunday, August 16, 2026

Paleolithic art • Family connection • Covadonga • Cantabrian coast • Gijón

This busy transfer day begins with a fixed 12:45 PM cave entry, then offers two memorable ways to reach Gijón. The featured route follows the family name to Tarano and continues to the Sanctuary of Covadonga. The alternate route stays closer to the coast for Gaudí, beaches or a Santoña anchovy experience.

12:45 PM — Cueva de El Pendo

The fixed point of the day: a UNESCO-listed cave with an extraordinary concentration of Paleolithic art.

Reservation: Entry is scheduled for 12:45 PM. Aim to arrive at least 20–30 minutes early for parking, tickets and the walk to the entrance.
Timed entryPrehistoric paintingsCool cave interiorNo-rush arrival

Keep a light layer accessible even in August, and confirm the cave’s current photography and bag rules before entering. This visit sets the tone for a day that moves from ancient human history into family history.

Tarano — The Family-Name Stop

A small waypoint with an outsized role in the story of the trip.

Photo mission: Find a village or road sign bearing the name Tarano, gather the family and take the photograph that will make this travel day unmistakably yours.

This need not be a long stop. The point is the connection: stepping into a place that echoes the family name before continuing deeper into Asturias.

Sanctuary of Covadonga

A dramatic historic and pilgrimage complex beneath the limestone mountains of the Picos de Europa.

BasilicaHoly CaveMountain settingAsturian history

Prioritize the central sanctuary rather than attempting to add the Covadonga Lakes to an already full transfer day. The basilica, Holy Cave and surrounding viewpoints provide a rewarding visit without turning the afternoon into an exhausting mountain excursion.

Time guardrail: Decide on a firm departure time for Gijón before leaving the cars. Covadonga invites lingering, but the final drive and hotel check-in still remain.

Alternate route

Cantabrian Coast Explorer

El Pendo → Gaudí, beaches or anchovies → Gijón

This route trades the personal Tarano–Covadonga story for architecture, sea air and food. Because the options spread in different directions, select one principal experience rather than trying to collect them all.

🎨 El Capricho de Gaudí

Continue west to Comillas for one of Gaudí’s exuberant early works. This is the most natural cultural substitute for the family route because it lies generally along the journey toward Gijón.

Morning-of check

Confirm the 12:45 PM El Pendo reservation and live driving conditions. For the family route, verify the exact Tarano pin, Covadonga access and parking. For the coastal route, confirm advance-ticket availability for El Capricho or the anchovy tour before leaving Santander.

Pack for transitions: Keep cave layers, walking shoes, water, snacks and phone chargers accessible rather than buried under the luggage.
