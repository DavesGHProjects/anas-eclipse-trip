Segovia

Three eras of Spain—Roman engineering, medieval royalty and late-Gothic grandeur—within one highly walkable old city.

Why it sells the stop: This is not merely another attractive old town. Few places let you stand beneath a monumental Roman aqueduct, walk through a living medieval city and finish at a castle perched like the prow of a ship—all within one visit.

Don’t miss

Under the aqueductCathedral squareAlcázar exteriorOld-town walk
Time-saving version: Aqueduct, taxi or drive toward the Alcázar area, then a concise old-town walk. Segovia can easily consume half a day, so agree on a departure time before dispersing.

Pedraza

A remarkably intact fortified village where the setting—not a single headline monument—is the attraction.

Why it sells the stop: Pedraza gives the group the sensation of entering a complete medieval environment. It is atmospheric, compact and easy to understand: walk through the gate, follow the stone street and arrive at one of Castile’s loveliest plazas.

Don’t miss

Puerta de la VillaPlaza MayorStone façadesCoffee or gelato
Group advantage: This is a high-reward stop without a strenuous itinerary. People can wander independently for 45–60 minutes and reconvene in the Plaza Mayor.

Sepúlveda

A dramatic hill town, a natural lunch break and the gateway to the Duratón canyon.

Why it sells the stop: Sepúlveda turns lunch into part of the journey. Its steep streets and elevated setting feel markedly different from Pedraza, while its location makes it the logical point to decide how ambitious the afternoon should be.

Don’t miss

Plaza de EspañaPanoramic outlookRomanesque characterRelaxed lunch
For your vegetarian: Traditional restaurants emphasize roast lamb, so confirm vegetarian choices before committing to an asador; salads, cheeses, eggs and vegetable dishes are generally the most realistic options.

Hoces del Río Duratón

The landscape payoff: a river twisting beneath ochre limestone cliffs, with griffon vultures riding the thermals overhead.

Why it sells the stop: After a day of architecture, the gorge supplies a complete change of scale and mood. Even the easy option gives the group a memorable natural-landscape finale; the longer option rewards photographers with the park’s iconic composition.

Riaza

The day’s exhale: a graceful Castilian town at the foot of the Sierra de Ayllón and your base for the next stage of the trip.

Why it matters: Riaza marks the transition from sightseeing to settling in. Its arcaded oval Plaza Mayor is attractive enough for an evening stroll, but the real appeal is arriving, unpacking and shifting into the slower rhythm of rural Spain.
