Arrival in Madrid

Airport reunions • Rental-van logistics • Groceries • A gentle landing

This is a recovery and regrouping day rather than a sightseeing day. The goal is to get both branches of the family together, collect the van without rushing, settle into the Airbnb and keep the evening easy.

Airport rendezvous

The principal challenge is not the drive—it is coordinating two international arrivals nearly five hours apart.

Best division of labor: The Miami group should not remain in baggage claim all morning. Once through formalities, they can find seats and food in the public arrivals area while staying in touch by group text.

Before landing

Share flight numbersConfirm arrival terminalsChoose one meeting pointKeep phones charged
Allow for variation: A scheduled landing time is not a curbside meeting time. Immigration, baggage delivery and oversized luggage can make actual emergence unpredictable.

Have each arriving party message the group only after all luggage is in hand. The van drivers can then give a precise pickup location rather than circling the terminals prematurely.

Rental-van pickup

Treat the handover as the day’s only important task.

At the counter: Confirm the exact terminal or shuttle procedure, all authorized drivers, fuel policy, toll arrangements and whether the vehicle can comfortably accept seven people plus every suitcase.

Before leaving the garage

Photograph every sideCheck roof heightPair one phoneSet the lodging routeLocate reverse camera
Madrid driving: Let one person navigate and another watch signs while the driver concentrates on the unfamiliar vehicle. Avoid improvising a central-city sightseeing loop on arrival day.

Settle in, then do less

The successful version of Day 1 ends with everyone fed, oriented and ready to sleep—not with a checklist of Madrid sights.

Groceries

Buy only breakfast basics, water, fruit, snacks and anything needed for the next morning. Because it is Sunday, verify a nearby market’s hours before sending the whole group out.

Dinner

Choose somewhere walkable or extremely close to the lodging. A casual café, tapas bar or neighborhood restaurant is preferable to a destination meal that requires another drive.

Energy rule: Anyone who wants fresh air can take a short walk; anyone who needs sleep should feel free to stay in. Keep the group plan flexible after two long travel days.
