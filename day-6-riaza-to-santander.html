Orbaneja del Castillo

A limestone village built around water: a spring emerges from the Cueva del Agua and tumbles through the settlement toward the Ebro.

Why this is the right lunch stop: It breaks the long drive with a destination that feels wholly different from Riaza—cool water, steep stone lanes and a dramatic canyon setting—without requiring a formal hike.

Best use of the stop

Lower cascade viewpointCueva del AguaStone-village lanesLunch with a firm departure time
Parking reality: Visitors generally park outside the village and approach on foot. Expect slopes, uneven paving and potentially busy parking in August. Keep unnecessary luggage out of sight and allow extra time to regroup at the cars.
About “Pozas de la Turquesa”: Treat this as the turquoise pools and terraces created by the same waterfall system, not as a separate major excursion. The safest plan is to enjoy the visible pools around the cascade rather than build in an additional hike.

Lunch strategy

Choose a restaurant soon after arrival and tell the staff that you have a timed museum booking. Traditional menus may lean toward meat; the ovo-lacto vegetarian in the group should look for salads, tortilla, cheeses, croquetas or vegetable dishes and confirm ingredients before ordering.

Altamira Museum & Neocave

A carefully reconstructed encounter with one of Europe’s defining Paleolithic art sites, paired with a museum devoted to the people and culture of Altamira.

Your reservation: Museum arrival at 5:30 PM; timed Neocave entry at 5:55 PM. Keep the booking confirmation readily available on at least two phones.
Do not aim for 5:30: Plan to park by about 5:00 PM. This leaves time for restrooms, ticket questions and walking from the parking area without rushing the group.

What you are seeing

Polychrome bison ceilingCave reconstructionPaleolithic lifewaysArchaeology exhibits

The Neocave is the public reconstruction; access to the fragile original cave is extraordinarily restricted. The museum is open into the evening from May through October on Tuesdays through Saturdays, but your timed reservation—not the general closing hour—should govern the day.

Realistic visit length: With a late entry, expect a focused visit rather than a slow, exhaustive museum afternoon. Prioritize the Neocave and the principal permanent exhibition, then leave enough energy for the final drive and hotel check-in.

Evening in Santander

The reward after a long inland-to-coast crossing: sea air, a waterfront paseo and a low-pressure first evening in Cantabria.

Arrival expectation: Depending on how long you remain at Altamira, hotel arrival will likely be in the early evening. Santander is farther from Altamira than Santillana del Mar, so allow approximately 30–40 minutes rather than ten.

Keep the evening simple

Check inWaterfront strollCentro Botín exteriorTapas or an easy dinner

After five hours or more behind the wheel plus two attractions, this is not the night for an ambitious sightseeing program. A short walk around the Jardines de Pereda and Centro Botín area gives an immediate sense of the city without creating another deadline.

Lodging in Santander

Four rooms at the Ibis Styles Santander give the group a straightforward hotel landing after the long drive and Altamira visit. With separate rooms already reserved, the main task on arrival is simply to park, distribute keys and luggage, and agree on whether dinner will be near the hotel or closer to the waterfront.

Before leaving Altamira: Confirm the hotel’s exact parking or drop-off instructions and identify who has the reservation confirmation. For a group arriving in a van, it is easier to settle this before entering Santander traffic.
