Day 4 · Wednesday, August 12

Eclipse Day & Ana’s Birthday

The big day: ANA’S BIRTHDAY! Oh, and there’s an eclipse. We’ll spend the day running around like headless chickens, then find a clear western sky for the evening’s main event.

Today’s Most Important Celestial Event

🎂 Happy Birthday, Ana! 🎂

A birthday so special that the Sun and Moon arranged their schedules around it.

The plan

Celebrate first. Chase the sky second.

The daytime itinerary is deliberately flexible. The only immovable deadline is being settled at a viewing spot with an unobstructed western horizon well before the eclipse begins.

Working strategy: enjoy Ana’s birthday, keep an eye on clouds, and be ready to pivot if the clearest sky is somewhere other than the original plan.

Most important logistical detail: the Sun will be very low. A wide, completely unobstructed view toward the west is more valuable than a picturesque site with hills, trees or buildings in that direction.
Bring

Viewing checklist

  • Certified eclipse glasses for everyone
  • Folding chairs or blankets
  • Warm layers for after sunset
  • Water and easy snacks
  • Birthday treats or a celebratory toast
  • Phones fully charged
  • Portable battery pack
  • Headlamps or flashlights
  • Insect repellent
  • Camera, tripod and spare batteries
  • Lens cloth
  • A simple backup viewing location
Before leaving

Five-minute departure check

  • Can we see clear sky to the west?
  • Is the chosen horizon free of mountains, trees and buildings?
  • Does every person have eclipse glasses in hand?
  • Are all cameras already set up and tested?
  • Do we know the backup location if clouds move in?
Rule of the evening: no one gets left behind because they went back for one more camera accessory.
Photography

Get the camera ready before the light starts changing

The eclipse is too short to spend totality navigating menus. Set up, focus and rehearse while the Sun is still bright.

Before the eclipse

  • Use a sturdy tripod.
  • Turn image stabilization off when the camera is tripod-mounted.
  • Use manual focus and magnify the image to focus carefully on the Sun’s edge.
  • Use a proper solar filter on the lens during all partial phases.
  • Test exposure and bracketing in advance.

During totality

  • Remove the solar filter only when totality has fully begun.
  • Bracket exposures rather than chasing one “perfect” setting.
  • Take a few photographs, then stop and look with your own eyes.
  • Replace the solar filter immediately as totality ends.
Best photograph of the day: it may be Ana and the family reacting to the eclipse, not the eclipse itself.
After the Sun sets

Birthday evening, continued

Once the eclipse is over, the pressure is off. Pack up carefully, count heads, and continue Ana’s birthday celebration somewhere warm, comfortable and well supplied with food.

Suggested final toast: “To Ana—whose birthday briefly turned off the Sun.”
